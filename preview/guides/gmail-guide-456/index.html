01The important decision

Google is the provider. Gmail is the product you’re connecting. Your assistant needs a Google OAuth client and permission to access Gmail—not a Google Play app or a Marketplace listing.

Reading an inbox changes the launch path.Send-only access and mailbox reading have different scope classifications. Decide what your assistant needs before configuring production credentials. See Google’s scope reference ↗

02Before you start

  • A Google Cloud project you can administer.
  • A clear list of Gmail features: send, read, search, or modify.
  • A development callback URL and a plan for production HTTPS.
  • For public verification: owned domains, a homepage, privacy disclosures, and a feature demonstration.

03Set up your OAuth app

1

Create a project and enable Gmail

Select your development project in Google Cloud and enable the Gmail API. Keep development and production configuration separate.

Open Google Cloud Console ↗
Outcome: your project can request Gmail API access.
2

Configure branding and audience

Add your app identity and support details in Google Auth Platform. Choose Internal for an eligible organization-only app, or External for users outside it. Use Testing for your pilot.

Audience requirements ↗
Outcome: Google knows who your app is and who can authorize it.
3

Choose the narrowest permissions

Map each feature to a Gmail scope. Send-only access can use gmail.send; reading messages may require gmail.readonly. Do not request full mailbox access by default.

Compare Gmail scopes ↗
Example: copy the send-only scope

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send

Google classifies this scope as sensitive. It does not permit reading mail.

4

Create the OAuth client and test the callback

For a server-side assistant, configure a web application client and its authorized redirect URI. Protect credentials, validate OAuth state, and test with an allowed test account. Check Google’s AI-agent client requirements for your architecture.

Outcome: a test user can authorize the intended Gmail access.

05What could delay launch?

Provider estimates, not guarantees. Security-assessment duration is not established here. Google’s verification FAQ ↗

What if my app reads or summarizes email?

Plan for restricted scopes. If you store or transmit restricted data on servers, Google documents a security-assessment requirement. Clarify how data reaches your model provider, how long you retain it, and how users can delete it.

Will every connection return a refresh token?

No. Offline access, prior consent, testing status, and token lifecycle rules affect refresh-token availability and validity. Test reconnect and revoked-access handling; do not assume indefinite access.

06What your users will experience

Connect Gmail→Google sign-in→Review permissions→Return to assistant

Your backend handles the authorization callback and token exchange. Your research agent provides setup guidance; it does not perform these runtime actions for users.

Your next action

Define the smallest useful Gmail feature.

Write down whether your assistant sends, reads, summarizes, or modifies email—and whether launch is internal, a pilot, or public. Those answers determine scopes and the approval path.

  • Developer: choose features, audience, and data handling.
  • Agent: help map scopes and prepare a review checklist.
  • Developer: sign in, configure credentials, and submit reviews.
